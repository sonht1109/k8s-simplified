kubectl Port-Forwarding

You deployed an app, but you cannot open it in your browser. kubectl port-forward creates a private tunnel from your machine to a Pod or Service. You will learn how it works, the three common situations, and how to fix the usual errors.

Services and NodePort are explained in Lesson 6 and Lesson 8. To run a local cluster like the one used here, see Lesson 26: kind.

Port forwarding in plain words

Port forwarding means "connect a port on my machine to a port on another machine". It is an old networking idea, not a Kubernetes invention. Classic example: a database on a server should only be reachable by company clients. To test it, you do not open the firewall to everyone; you create an SSH tunnel, so that localhost:3306 on your laptop secretly leads to port 3306 on the server. When you finish testing, you close the tunnel and nothing was ever exposed.

kubectl port-forward does the same for the cluster network.

Why you need it

Pods and ClusterIP Services live on a private network inside the cluster. Your laptop is outside it. Running Kubernetes in Docker (kind, minikube with the Docker driver) adds one more layer: the "node" is a container with its own network, so even a NodePort is often not reachable from your browser.

You could set up an Ingress (Lesson 9) or a LoadBalancer, but for a quick test that is too much work. Port-forward is the shortcut.

The command

kubectl port-forward <resource> <local-port>:<remote-port> [-n namespace]

The resource can be a Pod, a Deployment or a Service (pod/name, deploy/name, svc/name). They all end up at a container in the end. For a Service, the remote port is the Service port; for a Pod, it is the container's port. The command keeps running in your terminal; press Ctrl+C to close the tunnel.

Example: install Argo CD in a local kind cluster and open its web UI.

kubectl create namespace argocd
kubectl apply -n argocd -f https://raw.githubusercontent.com/argoproj/argo-cd/stable/manifests/install.yaml
kubectl get svc -n argocd                   # argocd-server is ClusterIP
kubectl port-forward svc/argocd-server -n argocd 9000:443

Now open https://localhost:9000 (accept the self-signed certificate warning). Changing the Service to NodePort would not have helped here, because the node is a container your laptop cannot reach.

Use case 1: cluster on your laptop

The default. kubectl and the browser are on the same machine, and the tunnel listens on 127.0.0.1 (localhost) only. That is also the safe choice, since nobody else on the network can use your tunnel.

Use case 2: cluster on a cloud VM

Say minikube runs on an EC2 instance or Azure VM and you want to use your laptop's browser. Run the port-forward on the VM, but tell it to listen on all network interfaces:

kubectl port-forward --address 0.0.0.0 svc/argocd-server -n argocd 9000:443

Then browse to https://<VM-public-IP>:9000. Remember to allow inbound port 9000 in the VM's security group or firewall. Without --address 0.0.0.0 the tunnel only listens on the VM's own localhost, which you cannot reach from outside.

Watch outBinding to 0.0.0.0 opens the tunnel to anyone who can reach that port. Restrict the firewall to your own IP and close the tunnel when finished. Do not leave it running on a production machine.

Use case 3: the local port is taken

If you ask for 9000:80 and something already uses 9000, you get an error like bind: address already in use. Two options: find and stop that process, or let kubectl choose a free port by leaving the local port empty:

kubectl port-forward svc/argocd-server -n argocd :443
# Forwarding from 127.0.0.1:62708 -> 8080

kubectl prints the port it picked. This is handy in scripts and CI pipelines where you cannot know which ports are free.

Troubleshooting

SymptomLikely cause and fix
address already in useLocal port taken. Pick another or leave it blank.
connection refused in the browser or in the kubectl outputWrong remote port: the app is not listening there. Check containerPort and the Service's port/targetPort.
Works on the VM, not from your laptopMissing --address 0.0.0.0, or the firewall blocks the port.
Tunnel stops by itselfThe Pod restarted or was replaced; a Pod-targeted tunnel dies with it. Re-run the command, or target the Deployment or Service.
unable to forward ... pod not runningThe Pod is Pending or crashing. Fix that first with kubectl get pods and describe.
https page shows a certificate warningNormal for self-signed certs on the app side; accept it for local tests.
TipPort-forward to a Service still connects to one Pod behind it; it does not load-balance. It is a debugging and testing tool, not a way to serve users.

Check yourself

What does kubectl port-forward svc/web 8080:80 do?
The format is local:remote. It changes nothing in the cluster; it only opens a tunnel.
Your cluster runs on an EC2 instance. You run port-forward there, but your laptop cannot connect. What is missing?
By default the tunnel listens only on localhost of the machine running kubectl.
How do you let kubectl pick a free local port?
With an empty local port kubectl chooses a free one and prints it.

Key takeaways

  • kubectl port-forward is a temporary tunnel from a local port to a Pod, Deployment or Service port.
  • Syntax: local:remote. It stays open until you press Ctrl+C.
  • On a remote VM add --address 0.0.0.0 and open the firewall, carefully.
  • Leave the local port empty (:80) to get a free random one.
  • It is for testing and debugging. Use Ingress or LoadBalancer for real traffic.
